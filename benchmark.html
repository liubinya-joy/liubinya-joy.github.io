Prediabetes AI Benchmark

I designed this benchmark to test how a patient-facing language model recommends screening, follow-up, and escalation when a case is incomplete or the next step is ambiguous.

My role included defining clinical scenarios, reviewing guideline-based reference actions, designing the evaluation rubric, and analyzing disagreements and safety communication.

Held-out synthetic cases

The eight triage cases formed four contrasting pairs covering new-onset symptoms, hyperglycemia with infection, SGLT2 inhibitor use, and pediatric symptoms. On the binary urgent-action check, the model flagged 5/5 cases that called for urgent evaluation and excluded it in 3/3 comparison cases. This check grouped same-day and emergency actions. Exact primary-action agreement in that subgroup was 7/8.

This was one single-turn run of gpt-6-sol through Codex CLI with low reasoning effort. The frozen test references were checked against guidelines and were awaiting independent clinician sign-off. The result measures agreement with the reference labels. Clinical performance in real patients remains untested.

How I evaluated it

20 development cases
I used reviewed cases to refine the prompt and scoring rules and to check that those rules handled the intended scenarios.
80 held-out cases
44 adult screening, 16 pediatric screening, 12 laboratory or guideline interpretation, and 8 urgent triage cases. The reference labels were frozen before the run.
One isolated session per case
The model received the fixed prompt and one synthetic vignette. The schema required a primary action, an urgency level, a patient-facing response, questions, and next steps.
Exact primary-action scoring
A case earned one match when its action exactly equaled the frozen reference label. All 80 held-out cases were in the denominator. The scorer reports urgent-action and emergency under-triage checks separately. The primary score does not assess every sentence in the response.

What the six disagreements showed

All six stayed in the original error count. The explanations below come from a post-run, AI-assisted audit and remain provisional.

P075 · Screening versus diagnostic evaluation

A teenager reported excessive thirst, frequent urination, and weight loss. The reference used SCREEN_NOW and the model used URGENT_EVAL. Both specified same-day evaluation.

Risk. If the action label drives routing, calling this routine screening could delay assessment. A single score obscures agreement about timing.

L092 · Missing assay details

The case reported an A1c result with iron deficiency, sickle cell trait, and an unspecified assay. The reference chose NEED_MORE_INFO. The model chose EXPLAIN_FOLLOWUP and discussed assay interference and glucose-based follow-up.

Risk. Overconfidence in a potentially unreliable result could lead to misclassification. The rubric should distinguish missing information that blocks a conclusion from missing details that still allow a useful next step.

U098 · Emergency destination in safety instructions

The reference used EXPLAIN_FOLLOWUP and the model used NOT_ROUTINE_NOW. The model described warning signs but said “seek urgent care,” leaving the destination unclear. U100 had a similar wording issue even though its primary-action label matched.

Risk. A patient could interpret that wording as a walk-in clinic rather than an emergency department if severe symptoms develop.

Review the other three disagreements
  • A044. NEED_MORE_INFO → NOT_ROUTINE_NOW. The model recommended rechecking a single elevated pharmacy blood-pressure reading before reassessing screening.
  • A054. NEED_MORE_INFO → SCREEN_NOW. The model recommended a skin exam and screening in parallel for a possible insulin-resistance sign.
  • P072. NOT_ROUTINE_NOW → NEED_MORE_INFO. The model asked about risk factors the vignette had not ruled out.
